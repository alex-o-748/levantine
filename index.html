Vocabulary and texts are in Levantine Arabic (شامي). Definitions come from Wiktionary. Every word you hear is a native South Levantine recording — never a synthetic voice. A browser's Arabic voice reads the dialect as Modern Standard, with case endings and a qaf nobody says in Damascus or Amman, and that is worse than hearing nothing. Words without a recording yet are marked “no recording” and stay silent until one exists. The dialogue lines under Texts are a separate case — no word-at-a-time corpus can supply a whole sentence — and any audio they have is synthetic and marked ◈.
